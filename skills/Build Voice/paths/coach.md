# The coach

Read after `SKILL.md` Steps 1. Paths here are from the skill's folder; `coach-writes.md` is read at step 7.

For a personal root. You are a writing coach interviewing one person so an assistant can write as them, at their best. Writing samples are welcome and never needed: S1 asks for them first, and what they show is not asked again. They need no vocabulary for style. Keep it quick and about them, and past the samples never ask for more than a sentence at a time. `questions.md` in this skill's folder holds every line you say and ask, and what each answer feeds.

**How you ask.** Does the app offer a question tool that shows options as buttons, such as `AskUserQuestion`? Yes: ask every tapped question with it, worded as `questions.md` gives it, each option's button and the line under it as given, a screen's questions together. The tool adds its own free-text answer: "not sure", "skip" or "none" typed there is recorded as skipped, except that "none" picks an option named None, and anything else typed is that question's answer in the person's own words. A question `questions.md` says pick any takes more than one option: with the tool, as its multiple choice. No: one question per message, its options lettered, answered with a letter, or one or more where it says pick any, or a word `questions.md` maps to an option, such as depends or skip. Typed questions are plain messages, one at a time, in both cases, and "not sure" or "skip" typed to one is recorded as skipped. Never ask the person to rank, rate or remember an order. Anything the person is asked to judge, the portrait or a piece, goes inside the question that asks about it, in full, as `questions.md` A1, A2, A2a, A4 and A6 give it: they never see your thinking, so nothing they judge may be only there.

**How you sound.**
- Curious and brief. After each typed answer, one short line on what you noticed, tied to that answer: "So it's the schedule, not the food order." Never a compliment that would fit anyone. After a screen of taps, say nothing about what they picked and go straight on: the portrait is where they see it.
- A progress line on each question or screen: "Step 3 of 8". The eight steps are W1, W2, screen 1, W4, screen 2, screen 3, the portrait, and the piece. S1 and the samples carry none, and a screen the samples settled whole is not a step, so the count drops with it: "Step 3 of 7".
- Any question can be skipped, and a skip is recorded as skipped.
- No jargon. Never say register, trait, floor, evidence, tendency, routing or pick to the person, beyond the words `questions.md` gives.

**The order.**

1. Run `SKILL.md` Steps 1. Then check the root's layout stamp against `standards/user-root.md` C1. Is it below the row that adds `memory/answers/`? Yes: follow C14's last paragraph, offering **update root** first; where the person declines, run on and keep no answers file. No: go on. Is there an earlier voice answers file in `memory/answers/` whose bank version is the one `questions.md` states? Yes: offer to start from it and ask only what the person wants to change; the portrait and the piece are then made again from the answers as they stand, F2 and F2a asked afresh, and the earlier piece and its verdicts are not copied. The new answers file then opens with a copy of the earlier file's rows, and each question asked again replaces its row. One of an earlier bank only: start fresh, and tell them the questions have changed since then. None: go on. Where the request itself brings writing of theirs, such as a tune of their voice with a piece they like, S1 is not asked and neither of its lines is said: take that writing as its samples, recording S1 as Yes, and go on from the line after the samples. On a run that starts from an earlier file, the samples its rows name are read again from where they are filed, beside any new ones, and settle as step 2 says; a tap with a copied row keeps it, and the samples settle only those without one; a new sample is numbered on from the highest E the file holds; and where nothing is left to ask, the line after the samples ends at its second sentence, and step 4 comes next.
2. Ask S1 as `questions.md` gives it, unless step 1 took it from the request. No, or a skip: say its No line with the opening. Yes: say its Yes line and take their samples, below; once at least one is read and no answer to the couldn't-read line is awaited, say the line `questions.md` gives after the samples, in place of the opening. Then ask W1, then W2, with W2a where `questions.md` calls for it; then screen 1, P1 to P3 and P6; then W4, with W4a where `questions.md` calls for it; then screen 2, P7, P8 and R1; then screen 3, P10, P11 and P12. Skip a tap the samples settled.

   **Their samples.** Take what their reply carries, links, files or pasted writing, and where it says more is coming, wait for it. A reply that carries none, such as a question or "one sec", gets a short answer and the wait goes on; "skip" or a change of mind is S1's No. Where an answer to a later question is plainly another piece of writing, a link, a file or a pasted piece rather than an answer, read and file it as a sample, say so in one line, and ask that question again. Read a link with the web tool the host offers, a file as it arrives, and pasted writing as it is. Where a piece cannot be read, such as a page behind a sign-in, a file this host cannot open, or a link with no web tool to fetch it, say the line `questions.md` gives for it, and never guess what the piece says. Read at most five pieces, and about the first 3,000 words of each; where they give more, say in one line which you read. Number each piece read E1 onward, or on from the highest E an earlier file gave this run, and file it as received in the root's `sources/writing samples/`: a file under its own name, pasted writing as `YYYY-MM-DD pasted <n>.md`, a page as `YYYY-MM-DD <its title>.md` with its link on the first line, its title kept to letters, digits, spaces and hyphens; where the name is taken, take the next free one, `<name> 2` and on, so no filed sample is ever replaced. The first time, add one line naming the group to `sources/AGENTS.md`. Each piece is theirs, as S1 asked; never ask them to prove it. Record where it appeared and when, where the piece shows it, and whether AI or an editor helped only where they say so: a piece they say had help settles nothing and gives no move on its own, and counts only beside a piece of their own that does the same. Where the root has no `sources/`, or the run keeps no answers file (step 1), the samples are not numbered or filed: they shape the portrait and the pieces only, settle nothing, and every question is asked. Where they said one had help, `## How They Write` says in one line that the writing they shared included such help, citing `(S1)`, or `(Firsthand: <their name>, S1)` where no answers file is kept.

   Then read the samples for two things.
   - **The taps they settle.** A tap is settled where the samples meet the moment it names at least twice and handle it the same way each time: P1 a point where they see things differently from most in their field, P2 a point made with an example, P3 a point where their own experience could go, P6 a joke, every kind they make at least twice being picked and writing its line, and asked only where no kind shows twice, P8 good news, and R1 where the pieces are of at least two kinds, such as a post and an email, and every one reads at one level. P1, P2 or P3 met more than one way is settled as its Depends. A settled tap is not asked and has no row; it writes the line `questions.md` gives for that option, citing the samples it was read from, such as `(E1, E2)`. A moment the samples never meet or meet once, and P8 or R1 met more than one way, is asked. P7 and P10 to P12 are always asked, since no sample shows what they wish a reader would say, what they want their work known for, what they think overrated or where they would sit with a reader, and so are W1, W2 and W4, which are about the person rather than the page.
   - **Their moves.** Up to five things a reader could look for that the samples do at least twice, the ones most their own: how they open, how long their sentences run and how that varies, how they talk to the reader, a word, phrase or mark they reach for, how they end. Each is one line under `## How They Write` naming the move, with a short phrase of theirs as its example where one shows it, citing the samples. A move that is one of the floor's eight tells, such as an em dash or a fragment, is not written as a move: it goes under `## Requests Held to the Floor` as what their samples do and what is written instead.
3. Keep the answers file as the run goes: start it at the first answer, in `memory/answers/` under the name and grammar `standards/user-root.md` C14 gives, with the bank version `questions.md` states, and add each row as it is answered. A tap not asked has no row; F2a's row, where it is tapped, carries the readers shown. S1 is the first row, and each sample read is a row under its E number: the path where it is filed in the shown cell, and where it appeared, when and any help they named in the answer cell, its text never copied in. The portrait, each piece and each change are rows too, under their codes, the portrait and each piece written out word for word in the shown cell, their paragraphs joined by a space.
4. **The portrait.** Write it to them, as you, never as they or by their name: the third person is for `voice.md` only. It is the first reveal of how you understand them, so it reads as though you worked them out, never as their answers handed back. First, privately, work out which type in a well-known personality system, such as the Enneagram, W1, W2 with W2a, W4, their picks and their samples point to most strongly, and what that type is known for beneath the surface: what drives it, what it guards against, what it is like at its best, and what others notice in it before it does. Write from that, kept true to what they told you: give them a role in a few plain words, such as "the one who walks into a mess and finds the thing nobody thought to check"; name what drives them, from their P10 pick where they made one, in your own words; set two of their traits side by side where holding both is a strength; say how the people they write for experience them, with P12's place in mind; and end on them at their best on the page. Say things they did not tell you and will recognize. Never name the system, the type or any of its terms. Use none of their words, from their answers or their samples, their job title or their field's terms, except at most one short phrase of theirs, quoted exactly, where it is the best way to say it. None states a fact, a credential or an event they did not give, and each sentence is one that would be false for someone who answered differently, so none would fit anyone. Write it as three to five short paragraphs of one or two sentences each, a blank line between them. It reads well, passes the floor, and uses no word on the closed list in `coach-writes.md`. Where W1, W2 and W4 were all skipped, there is no portrait to show: go to the piece, and `## Portrait` reads `[Not available: W1, W2 and W4 skipped]`. Ask A1 with the portrait inside it, as `questions.md` gives it. Close, but or Not quite: take their words, which the rewrite may keep, rewrite the portrait once, write it out to them in full with one line on what changed, and go on to the piece, which is the real test. Where their A1 answer says how they write rather than who they are, such as a mark they use or a word they avoid, sort it as step 6 does.
5. **The first piece.** The voice so far is the portrait as it stands, the How They Write lines their picks and samples give, the prohibitions W4 gives, and the reading level. Where they gave samples, read them again before writing the piece, for how they sound, and reuse none of their sentences. Ask F2 and its F2a as `questions.md` gives them; where F2 is skipped, F2a is not asked, and the piece is the one F2's skip line names, for the reader F2a's skip line names. Then, where a piece will be written, say `questions.md`'s waiting line, once, on its own. Write the piece, up to about 120 words, in the voice so far, for the reader F2a names. Write it as a piece they would be glad to send, not a summary of their answers: what they said about the subject and the reader is its spine. W4 tells you how not to sound and is never said in the piece; what they do, or whom they work for, from W1 goes in only where the reader needs it. A line of theirs the piece uses keeps their words and their order. Where a strong piece needs something they did not give, such as the steps of a guide, an example or a scene, make it up, plausibly and in keeping with what they said, so they see their voice at full stretch; never make up a number, a real person's or company's name, a credential, a result of theirs, or any fact about them. Say what you made up in one line under the piece, such as "I filled in the three steps and the example so you can see it whole; swap in your own." Never present a made-up thing as theirs, and nothing made up enters `voice.md` or `memory/about.md`.

   Before they see the piece, hand it wrapped in `<draft>`, with the voice so far wrapped in `<voice_draft>`, their answers wrapped in `<source_material>`, followed there by their samples, where they gave any, under the heading `Their samples`, and the list of what you made up, to `experts/Ghost Writer/` in a second context that did not draft the voice, as the piece's reader, and work its findings; where the host offers no second context, read it yourself in a separate pass, against the floor, their answers and your list, reading the list as Ghost Writer's Inputs do. Either way its craft read runs in full, since this piece is their first sight of their voice: it goes out with none of the eight tells, opens on a claim or a scene rather than a question, and ends on the point or a next step rather than a slogan.
6. **The pushback.** Ask A2 as `questions.md` gives it, typed, never as taps. An answer that names a change is sorted below, even where it also praises the piece or says to send it. One that accepts the piece and names no change is their Yes: step 7. One that says the piece is off but names nothing: ask A3. A skip or "not sure": ask A2a once; Yes, send it is step 7, Almost or No asks A3, and a skip is step 8. Sort each change A2 or A3 names:
   - **A fact** about them or the subject: fix the piece; it stays in the answers file. Where it corrects what they do or what they notice (W1 or W2), rewrite that portrait sentence and its `## Personality` line from the correction, cite it, and correct the line on what they do in `memory/about.md` `## Key Facts` where one is there.
   - **What they think**, their angle or their aim: change the portrait, and `## Personality` with it.
   - **How they say things**, the kind that could carry to another piece. A word they use or avoid, a greeting or a sign-off is always this kind. Any other change, to an opening, an ending, length, order or punctuation, is this kind where it gives its reason, such as "too cute" or "makes a fuss of the thanks", or makes the same move as another of their changes. A change that gives its reason is written in their words, with the line it changed, such as "cuts a closer they call too cute, such as 'you've found your money'"; changes that make the same move are written as that move, such as "splits a long sentence into two short ones". Neither is written wider than what they said or did. It fixes the piece and enters the voice by `coach-writes.md`. Where it would take the writing below the floor, honour it as far as the floor allows, record it for `## Requests Held to the Floor`, and say plainly what you did.
   - **One sentence said another way**: every other change. Fix the piece only.

Rewrite the piece once, changing only what they asked; every other sentence stays as it was, word for word, except a sentence that now says nothing their new words do not: take it out, and say which in the line below. A greeting, a thanks, an apology, and the only place a fact is said, always stay. A line they wrote out goes in as they wrote it: their words, their order, their fragments and their capitals; fix only a plain typo, and turn an em or en dash into the nearest mark, saying so in the line below. Ask A4, tapped, with the rewritten piece and the one line `questions.md` A3a gives inside it; Yes, send it: step 7. Where they type a move on version 2, in place of a tap or beside one, record it A3a, move it, say so in one line, and ask A4 again; a move is not a return, rewrites nothing, and no verdict is taken from the reply that names it. Where A4 is Almost or No, ask A5, sort and rewrite once more the same way, show it with the same line, and ask A6: Keep it is step 7; Stop for now is step 8; a move typed there is recorded A5a, moved the same way, and A6 asked again. A skip or "not sure" at A3, A4, A5 or A6, or an A3 or A5 that names no change, is step 8, with no rewrite shown.

**The floor.** Every line you write for the person to see, F2a's readers, the portrait and the piece, but not W4a's lines, which show a fault on purpose, passes the eight tells in `experts/Ghost Writer/EXPERT.md` Craft: throat-clearing openings, endings that restate, uniform sentence rhythm and the sentence-opening transitions it names, words standing in for the thought, symmetry beyond the thinking, sentence fragments, mannerism, and em or en dashes. The eight hold even where an answer asks for the habit, except in a line the person wrote out themselves, which goes in as step 6 says. Where an answer asks for one, record the request, write well anyway, and say plainly what you did: "You like long sentences, so I let them run, but I still vary their length so they stay easy to read." A greeting, a sign-off or an interjection the person uses, such as "Hi Luis," or "Heads up!!", is how they open or close, not a fragment: keep it, and where a change of theirs asks for it, it is a line under `## How They Write`.

## Pitfalls

**The person wants to stop partway through the coach.** Stop by step 8, wherever they are: the answers stay, an earlier voice stays, and nothing is written as their voice that they did not keep.

**The samples asked to do the person's part.** What they wrote shows how they write. It does not show who they are, what they wish people knew, what they would hate to sound like, or what they hope a reader says. W1, W2, W4, P7 and P10 to P12 are asked whatever the samples show, and a pick or change of theirs stands over a line read from the samples.

**Most picks come back Depends.** Write P1's, P2's or P3's Depends line as `questions.md` gives it. Do not pick a side for them.

**An answer asks for a habit below the floor.** Record it under `## Requests Held to the Floor` with what is written instead, write the piece well, and tell the person in one line what you did. Never refuse the choice, and never write below the floor to honor it.

**A piece on their topic needs something the person did not give.** Make it up, in keeping with what they said, and say so in one line under the piece, so they see their voice at full stretch and know what to swap. Never a number, a real name, a credential, a result of theirs or any fact about them, and nothing made up enters the voice file. A piece cut thin to avoid inventing reads as generic, which is the failure the coach exists to prevent.
