# The interview

What the coach says and asks, in the order `SKILL.md` The coach runs it. A tapped question is asked with the app's question tool where it has one: each option a button, and where an option below has words after a colon, the words before it are the button and the words after it are the line under it. Where the app has none, one question per message, its options lettered, answered with a letter. A typed question is a plain message, one at a time. The codes (W, P, R, F, A) are the question numbers the answers file records and `voice.md` cites. Bank version for every row: `Build Voice bank 5`.

Each question says what it **feeds**. A line in quotation marks after a pick is the exact line that pick writes into `voice.md` `## How They Write`, copied word for word.

## Opening

> We are going to begin by building a writing style we call your "voice" together. It takes about eight minutes: a few questions about you, some quick taps, then I'll show you how you come across and write your first piece. There's nothing to prepare, and "not sure" is always a fine answer.

## W1 (typed)

> What do you do, and what do people come to you for? A few words is plenty, like "I teach chemistry, and people ask me to explain things simply."

Feeds: the portrait, never with their role or title; P3's detail; `about.md` `## Key Facts`, written by Onboard Root.

## W2 (typed)

> What's one thing about your work, or your world, you wish more people knew? An example helps, if one comes to mind.

"None" or a skip is a fine answer. Feeds: the portrait's angle; `about.md` `## Personality`; the wording of P1, P2 and P3; the piece on their topic at F2.

### W2a (typed)

Asked at most once, after W2, only when the answer is a label, a word or two, rather than a point. [what they do] is what they do, from W1, in their words, such as "doing the books for restaurants", or "your work" where W1 was skipped.

> I need you to expand on that a little: give me a full sentence about your point of view or a perspective you have on [what they do].

Feeds: whatever W2 feeds, made concrete.

## Screen 1 (tapped)

Said before it, leaving out its last two sentences where the screen asks no pair:

> Now some quick taps. These are simple: just pick the one you'd actually say, not the one that sounds better. "Both" is a real answer.

**How every pair is written.** Each pair is written from the person's own answer named under it; where that answer makes more than one point, from the first point it makes. Copy one stretch of what they said onto both sides, in their words, with their facts and their hedges, and change only the one thing the pair names: P1 the frame, P2 the order of their example and their point, P3 one detail of their own. Every word on either side is theirs except P1's frame and what a sentence needs to be whole. A side that leaves something out uses only words already in that stretch, and neither side changes who did what. Both sides pass the floor. Where the pair would need a second change, or anything they did not say, a fact, number, name, hedge, feeling, joke or image, the pair is not asked: it writes nothing, and a screen asks only the pairs their answers give. A pair never puts words in their mouth. The answers file records both lines shown.

Every pair is worded **"Which would you actually say?"** and has four options: its A line, its B line, **Both sound like me**, and **Neither**. With the app's question tool each line is its own whole button, with no letter and nothing under it; with none, the four are lettered as every question is. Both writes the pair's Both line. Neither, or a skip, writes nothing. Typed instead of tapped, "both" or "depends" is Both, "neither" is Neither, and "not sure" or "skip" is a skip.

### P1. Disagreeing

From W2's point, in their words. A says it outright; B puts the same words as their own view, with "I think" or "I'd". Where W2 gave no point, or that stretch already says they are sure, such as "I know", it is not asked.

Writes:
- A: "When disagreeing, says so outright, then gives the reason."
- B: "When disagreeing, puts it as their own view, then gives the reason."
- Both: "When disagreeing, says so outright or as their own view, as the piece needs."

### P2. Explaining

From W2's point and the example they gave with it, both in their words. A leads with the example, then the point; B leads with the point, then the example. Where W2 gave no example, or the example is not a case of the point, it is not asked.

Writes:
- A: "When explaining, starts from one concrete case, then the point."
- B: "When explaining, starts from the point, then the case that shows it."
- Both: "When explaining, starts from the case or the point, as the piece needs."

### P3. Your own experience

From W2's point and one detail of their own they gave in W1 or W2: the place they work, the people they work with, a thing they do. A makes the point with that detail; B is the same sentence without it. Never a stock opener such as "I've found", "I notice", "In my work" or "To me". Where they gave no such detail, it is not asked.

Writes:
- A: "Makes a point through their own experience when they have a true one to tell."
- B: "Keeps the point on the subject rather than on themselves."
- Both: "Uses their own experience or keeps to the subject, as the piece needs."

### P6. Humour

P6 to P9 are not pairs: each is the same for everyone and asked on every run, and each line it writes covers only the moment its question names. A skip writes nothing. Typed instead of tapped, an answer that plainly matches an option writes that option's line; one that matches none, such as "only about my own mistakes", writes one line in their words, for the same moment, cited with the tap's number.

> To help me gauge your usage of humor, tell me: when something's gone a bit wrong, would you make a joke about it in writing?

- Yes, it keeps everyone sane
- Only with people I know well
- No, not in writing

Writes:
- Yes, it keeps everyone sane: "When something has gone wrong, lets a light joke in."
- Only with people I know well: "When something has gone wrong, jokes about it only with readers they know well."
- No, not in writing: "When something has gone wrong, keeps jokes out of it."

## W3 (typed)

> How would a friend describe the way you talk? Their words, not the polite version.

Feeds: the portrait's sentence on how they come across; `about.md` `## Personality`.

## W4 (typed)

> And what would you hate to sound like? A word or phrase that makes you wince is perfect.

Feeds: `## Prohibitions`, as concrete phrases or moves; `about.md` `## Personality`.

### W4a (tapped)

Asked only when W4 names a kind of writing, such as "corporate" or "a finance textbook", rather than quoting words of it. Write three lines of that kind, one short sentence each, about the person's own work from W1, each a different phrase. They show the fault on purpose, so the floor does not hold them to it, and none has an em or en dash.

> Which of these would make you wince most?

- the first line
- the second line
- the third line
- None of these

Feeds: `## Prohibitions`, the line they tap as the example of what W4 names. None of these, or a skip, adds nothing beyond W4. The answers file records the three lines shown and the one tapped.

## Screen 2 (tapped)

Said before it:

> A few more taps, then I'll show you how you come across.

### P9. The stock phrase

> Your field has a phrase everyone uses that you find a bit ridiculous. In writing, would you poke fun at it?

- Yes, happily
- Only with people who'd get it
- No, I'd just never use it
- My field doesn't really have one

Writes:
- Yes, happily: "Will poke fun at a stock phrase of their field, rather than only leave it out."
- Only with people who'd get it: "Pokes fun at a stock phrase of their field only for readers in that field, and otherwise leaves it out."
- No, I'd just never use it: "Leaves the stock phrases of their field out quietly rather than mocking them."
- My field doesn't really have one: nothing.

### P7. What they'd love to hear

> Someone just finished reading something you wrote. What would you most love them to say?

- "That was so clear."
- "You made me laugh."
- "I felt that."
- "OK, you've convinced me."

Writes:
- That was so clear: "Where a piece has room, aims first for it to be clear."
- You made me laugh: "Where a piece has room, aims first to make the reader laugh."
- I felt that: "Where a piece has room, aims first for the reader to feel it."
- OK, you've convinced me: "Where a piece has room, aims first to persuade."

### P8. Excitement

> You've got good news to share. Does your excitement show?

- Yes, let it show
- A little
- No, the news speaks for itself

Writes:
- Yes, let it show: "Lets their excitement show when they have good news."
- A little: "Shows a little excitement at good news, never more than the news."
- No, the news speaks for itself: "Lets good news speak for itself, with no added excitement."

### R1. How should your writing usually read?

- Easy and casual: like a text to a friend
- Everyday: clear to anyone outside your field
- Polished: careful, for readers who expect it
- Technical: for people who know the terms

"Depends" typed in the free-text answer is recorded as answered. Feeds: `## Reading Ease`, the option's level and the line under it, or on Depends or a skip, "Set by each piece's reader"; the routing row's register.

## A1. The portrait (tapped)

The question carries the portrait, so the two are always seen together: it opens with "Here's how you come across on the page:", then the portrait in full, then:

> Is that you on a good day?

- Yes, that's me
- Close, but...
- Not quite

On Close, but or Not quite, they say what to change, in the free-text answer or a reply. Feeds: the portrait; `about.md` `## Personality` where it changes their angle or their aim; where it says how they write rather than who they are, it is sorted like an A3 change.

## F1. The reading (typed)

> Now let's have a little fun with your first writing sample. I'll write a personality profile of you in the style of a horoscope. Tell me your:
>
> - Birthday with year
> - State / province / locality where you were born
> - Roughly what time you were born
>
> Say "skip" if you have an allergy to hearing about yourself (but you'll be missing out on a little magic if you do).

The answer is recorded as given; a time they do not know is left out, and the rest still makes the reading. Skipped, or given without a birthday or a place: F2. Feeds: the reading's prompt, and nothing else.

The prompt, filled in and handed over as `SKILL.md` The coach step 5 says, word for word:

> I want you to give me an astrology reading for [name] born on [date] in [location] at [time] - focus on personality, career, ambition, and work/personal relationships. Keep the astrology terminology to a minimum

## F2. A piece on your topic (tapped)

Only after F1 is skipped or gives no birthday or no place, the reading does not come back, or A2a or A4a. Where W2 gave a point, F2 is not asked: the piece is the short post on what they wish people knew, and F2a asks its post line. Otherwise:

> Let's see it in action. What should I write first?

- Something I'm working on
- An email asking for a meeting
- A thank-you note to someone who helped

Skipped: a short note saying what they do, from W1; where W1 was skipped too, there is nothing to write from, and the run stops by `SKILL.md` The coach step 8.

## F2a (typed)

One sentence that gives the piece what it needs, asked by the piece:
- The post: "Who should read it, and what should they take away? A sentence is plenty."
- Something I'm working on: "What is it, and who's it for? A sentence is plenty."
- The meeting email: "Who's it to, and what's the meeting about?"
- The thank-you note: "Who's it for, and what did they do?"

Skipped: write only from what they told you in W1 and W2, for someone who would follow their work; where W1 and W2 were skipped too, there is nothing to write from, and the run stops by `SKILL.md` The coach step 8. Feeds: the piece's subject and reader; `about.md` `## Current Focus` where it is their work, written by Onboard Root.

## A2. The verdict (tapped)

The question carries the piece, so the two are always seen together: it opens with "Here's your first piece:", then the piece in full and, where anything in it was made up, the one line that says what, then the question for the piece.

After the reading:

> How does this piece sound?

- This is spooky good
- The writing style is on, personality is off
- Neither the style nor the personality works

After a piece on their topic:

> Would you send this under your name?

- Yes, send it
- Almost: I'd change a few things
- No, it doesn't sound like me

Each piece opens in short paragraphs with a blank line between them. After A2a or A4a, the piece on their topic opens with "Here's your voice on something you'd send:" in place of "Here's your first piece:", takes the verdict for a piece on their topic, Yes, Almost or No, and is recorded A2b. The answers file records each piece and what was made up. This is spooky good, or Yes, sets the confirmation date. After a rewrite it is asked again, in the same form, and recorded A4, opening with "Here it is with your changes:", the piece in full and the one line A3a gives.

### A2a (typed)

Asked on The writing style is on, personality is off, recorded A4a after a rewrite.

> What's off about it? A few words is plenty.

Feeds: the portrait and `about.md` `## Personality`; where it says how they write, a line in the voice. Then the piece on their topic, F2, with its verdict recorded A2b; a skip goes there too.

## A3 (typed)

Asked on Neither, Almost or No:

> What would you change? Quote the line and say it your way, or just tell me.

After the rewrite, A5. Feeds: each change by its sort in `SKILL.md`.

## A3a (typed, only when they answer it)

Never asked as a question. Version 2 is shown with one line on where each change went, naming what is now in their voice and what stays in this piece, and ending:

> Tell me if any of those should go the other way.

Where they name one, typed in place of a tap or in a reply, it is recorded as A3a and moved: into the voice, one line in `## How They Write`, or in `## Prohibitions` where it says never; or out of it, so the piece keeps it and the voice does not. Then A4 is asked again, and a move is not a return. Silence moves nothing. After the second rewrite, A5a.

## A6. Keep or stop (tapped)

Asked with the third version, after two returns, in place of A2, and carrying that version and its line as A4 does; or, after A4a, with the piece on their topic rewritten once from A5.

> Want to keep your voice as it is now, or stop here and pick it up later?

- Keep it
- Stop for now

## Close

> Your voice is saved in your project folder. Whenever you have something you wrote and like, bring it to me and ask me to tune your voice using it.
