# The question bank

The coach's questions, in the order `SKILL.md` The coach runs them. Every question has a plain-text form; where the app offers choice buttons, the coach may use them. The codes (W1, Y1, P1, R1, H1, S1, A1, X1) are the question numbers the answers file records and `voice.md` cites. Bank version for every row: `Build Voice bank 1`.

## Warm-up

**W1. What do you write most? Pick all that apply, with the one you write most first.**
1. Social posts
2. Emails to colleagues
3. Notes or proposals to clients
4. Articles or blog posts
5. Talks or presentations
6. Bios or profiles
7. Something else (say what)

**W2. Who reads it most? Pick one.**
1. People who know me well
2. Colleagues
3. Clients or customers
4. The public
5. Specialists in my field

**W3. What are you working on right now? A sentence is plenty.** Open. It becomes the finale's subject and the Current Focus in `about.md`.

## About you

Introduced as: "Ten quick ones about you, so I know who I'm writing for. Your answers tell me how you come across, and I'll say what I heard at the end. Answer each 1 to 5, where 1 is very inaccurate and 5 is very accurate. Skip any you don't want to answer. You can reply with five numbers at once, like 4 2 5 3 1."

Say each item's sentence only; the trait in brackets is for scoring and is never said to the person.

The five-point scale: 1 Very inaccurate, 2 Moderately inaccurate, 3 Neither accurate nor inaccurate, 4 Moderately accurate, 5 Very accurate.

The items are from the Mini-IPIP (Donnellan, Oswald, Baird and Lucas, 2006), as keyed at `ipip.ori.org/MiniIPIPKey.htm`, read 2026-10-21. IPIP items are in the public domain (`ipip.ori.org`). The trait each item keys is in brackets, and R marks a reverse-keyed item.

First batch:
- Y1. I am the life of the party. [Extraversion]
- Y2. I sympathize with others' feelings. [Agreeableness]
- Y3. I get chores done right away. [Conscientiousness]
- Y4. I get upset easily. [Neuroticism]
- Y5. I have a vivid imagination. [Imagination]

Second batch:
- Y6. I keep in the background. [Extraversion, R]
- Y7. I am not really interested in others. [Agreeableness, R]
- Y8. I make a mess of things. [Conscientiousness, R]
- Y9. I am relaxed most of the time. [Neuroticism, R]
- Y10. I have difficulty understanding abstract ideas. [Imagination, R]

Scoring: reverse an R item as 6 minus the answer; a trait's score is the mean of its two items, or the one item answered where the other was skipped, and no score where both were skipped. Steadiness is 6 minus the Neuroticism score. Bands: below 2.5 lower, 2.5 to 3.5 middle, above 3.5 higher.

Plain names: Outgoing (Extraversion), Considerate (Agreeableness), Organized (Conscientiousness), Steady (6 minus Neuroticism), Imaginative (Imagination). Never "neuroticism" to the person.

## The pairs

P1 to P8 from `pairs.md`, each asked as: "Which sounds more like you? Reply A, B, or in between." Odd pairs show A first, even pairs show B first. In between is recorded as such.

## Reading ease

**R1. Which reading level fits most of what you write?**
1. More approachable, less formal
2. Most common (the usual pick)
3. More formal
4. Technical
5. Not sure, explain these

On 5, the coach answers with each level's reader in one line, then asks R1 again:
- More approachable: a busy friend outside your field, reading on a phone.
- Most common: a smart colleague from another department.
- More formal: a client or executive who expects polish.
- Technical: a peer in your field who knows the terms.

## Adaptive pairs and the repeat

After S1, the coach writes up to three adaptive pairs, and one repeat:
- **Adaptive (A1 to A3):** one for each fixed pair the person answered "in between", taken in pair order, at most three. Written on the person's own W3 subject, in the kind of piece W1's first pick names, both sides above the floor, the same length within about ten percent, and differing on that pair's dimension alone. None is owed where no pair came back in between.
- **The repeat (X1):** P1's dimension, or P3's where P1 came back in between, or P4's where both did; none where all three did. Rewritten in a different situation: a social post, unless W1 picked social posts, then a note to a client. Where the answer flips, the coach asks which situation fits each side, and the routing table carries the difference.
