# The question bank

The coach's questions, in the order `SKILL.md` The coach runs them. Every question is a tap: asked with the app's question tool where it has one, each option a button, at most four options to a question and four questions to a screen; where the app has none, the plain-text form is the same options, answered with a letter. Where an option below has words after a colon, the words before it are the button and the words after it are the line shown under it. The codes (W1, Y1, P1, R1, H1, S1, A1, X1, F1) are the question numbers the answers file records and `voice.md` cites. Bank version for every row: `Build Voice bank 2`.

## Warm-up

**W1. Who do you write to most?**
- People I know well
- My team
- Clients or customers
- Lots of people at once (posts, articles, talks)

## About you

Introduced as: "Five quick picks about you, so I know who I'm writing for. No right answers; tap whichever is more you."

Each pick is two everyday choices. The first option is the higher end of the trait in brackets, which is for scoring and never said to the person.

- Y1. Friday night: A dinner with a crowd, or A quiet night in. [Outgoing]
- Y2. In a group decision: Keep everyone on board, or Push for the best answer. [Considerate]
- Y3. Your desk right now: Tidy, or Creative chaos. [Organized]
- Y4. Plans change at the last minute: Roll with it, or Need a minute. [Steady]
- Y5. Your ideal trip: Somewhere new, no plan, or A favorite place I know well. [Imaginative]

Scoring: the first option is higher, the second lower, and "both" or a skip leaves the trait unread. These picks are written for this coach, after the five broad traits personality research uses; they are not a validated test, and the coach says so in the voice card.

## The pairs

P1 to P8 from `pairs.md`, two pairs to a screen, each asked as: "Which sounds more like you?" with the options A, B and Both, each passage shown whole with its letter. Both is recorded as in between.

## Reading ease

**R1. How should your writing read?**
- Easy and casual: a busy friend reading on a phone
- Everyday, the usual pick: a smart colleague from another team
- Polished: a client or an executive who expects care
- Technical: a peer who knows the terms

## Hard lines

## Samples

**S1. Got any writing you're proud of? Totally optional.**
- Yes, I'll paste it or point you to it

## Adaptive pairs and the repeat

After S1, the coach writes up to three adaptive pairs, and one repeat, each two or three sentences a side:
- **Adaptive (A1 to A3):** one for each fixed pair the person answered Both, taken in pair order, at most three. A short email to a colleague on a new subject, both sides above the floor, the same length within about ten percent, and differing on that pair's dimension alone. None is owed where no pair came back Both.
- **The repeat (X1):** P1's dimension, or P3's where P1 came back Both, or P4's where both did; none where all three did. Rewritten as a short social post. Where the answer flips, the coach asks which fits each, and the routing table carries the difference.

## The first piece

**F1. Last one: what should I write for you first?**
- A thank-you note to someone who helped
- An email asking for a meeting
- A short post sharing some news
- Something I'm working on (tell me in a sentence)

The answer is the subject of the finale piece. Only the fourth, in their own words, is a fact about their work.
