# The interview

What the coach says and asks, in the order `paths/coach.md` runs it. A tapped question is asked with the app's question tool where it has one: each option a button, and where an option below has words after a colon, the words before it are the button and the words after it are the line under it. Where the app has none, one question per message, its options lettered, answered with a letter. A question that says pick any takes more than one of its options: with the question tool, as its multiple choice; with none, answered with one or more letters. A typed question is a plain message, one at a time. The codes (S, W, P, R, F, A) are the question numbers the answers file records and `voice.md` cites, and each sample the person gives is numbered E1 onward. Bank version for every row: `Build Voice bank 10`.

Each question says what it **feeds**. A line in quotation marks after a pick is the exact line that pick writes into `voice.md` `## How They Write`, copied word for word. A tap the person's samples settled is not asked, and a screen left with no question is not shown, nor the line said before it.

## S1. Your writing (tapped)

The coach's first question, said after any offer `paths/coach.md` step 1 makes and before anything else. Where the request itself brings their writing, it is not asked: the writing is its samples.

> Do you have examples of content you've written that you want to use to help craft your Wiser voice?

- Yes
- No

No, or a skip: say this line and the opening together, in one message.

> Okay, let's walk you through setting up your writing style (we call that voice in Wiser).

Yes:

> Great! You can give me those samples now. Either give me some URLs, drop the sample files directly here in chat, or paste the writing in chat.

Each piece that arrives is a sample, numbered E1 onward, or on from an earlier file's highest E, read and filed as `paths/coach.md` step 2 says. Feeds: the portrait, the pieces, `## How They Write`, and every tap the samples settle.

Where a piece cannot be read, said once in the run, naming each piece that could not be read, and then wait for their answer:

> I couldn't read [which one, and why, in a few words]. Paste the writing here instead, or say "skip" and we'll go on without it.

Where nothing has been read and they skip, or what they send next cannot be read either, go on as S1's No, with its line and the opening. Where at least one piece has been read, a piece that still cannot be read, or a skip, is left out, said in one line. Once at least one sample is read and nothing is waiting on that answer, say this line in place of the opening:

> Thanks, I've read [them, or it for one]. [One line on what stood out in their writing.] Now a few questions your writing can't answer, then I'll show you how you come across and write your first piece. "Not sure" is always a fine answer.

## Opening

Said with S1's No line.

> It takes about 15 minutes: a few questions about you, some quick taps, then I'll show you how you come across and write your first piece. There's nothing to prepare, and "not sure" is always a fine answer.

Feeds: the portrait, never with their role or title; F2a's readers; `about.md` `## Key Facts`, written by Onboard Root.

## W2 (typed)

"None" or a skip is a fine answer. Feeds: the portrait's angle; `about.md` `## Personality`; F2a's readers; the piece on their topic at F2.

### W2a (typed)

Asked at most once, after W2, only when the answer is a label, a word or two, rather than a point. [what they do] is what they do, from W1, in their words, such as "doing the books for restaurants", or "your work" where W1 was skipped.

> I need you to expand on that a little: give me a full sentence about your point of view or a perspective you have on [what they do].

Feeds: whatever W2 feeds, made concrete.

## Screen 1 (tapped)

Said before it:

> Now some quick taps. These are simple: just pick what's true of you, not what sounds better.

P1 to P3, P6 to P8 and P10 to P12 are the same for everyone. P7 and P10 to P12 are asked on every run, and the rest on every run whose samples did not settle them; each line a tap writes covers only the moment its question names. A skip writes nothing. Typed instead of tapped, an answer that plainly matches an option writes that option's line, "depends" being the option that starts Depends; one that matches none, such as "only about my own mistakes", writes one line in their words, for the same moment, cited with the tap's number.

### P1. Seeing it differently

> When you see things differently from most people in your field, how does it show in your writing?

- I call it out: name what's wrong and why
- I show a better way: no argument, just the alternative
- I build on common ground: start where we agree
- Depends on who's reading

Writes:
- I call it out: "Challenges the usual view openly: names what's wrong and why."
- I show a better way: "Shows a better way rather than arguing with the usual one."
- I build on common ground: "Starts from what the reader already agrees with, and rarely picks a fight."
- Depends on who's reading: "Challenges the usual view, shows a better way, or builds on common ground, as the reader needs."

### P2. Explaining

> When you explain something, where do you start?

- With an example: then what it shows
- With the point: then an example
- Depends on the piece

Writes:
- With an example: "When explaining, starts from one concrete case, then the point."
- With the point: "When explaining, starts from the point, then the case that shows it."
- Depends on the piece: "When explaining, starts from the case or the point, as the piece needs."

### P3. Your own story

> When you make a point, do you bring in your own story?

- Often: a moment from my own work or life
- Rarely: I keep it on the subject
- Depends on the piece

Writes:
- Often: "Makes a point through their own experience when they have a true one to tell."
- Rarely: "Keeps the point on the subject rather than on themselves."
- Depends on the piece: "Uses their own experience or keeps to the subject, as the piece needs."

### P6. Humour

> What kind of humor shows up in your writing? Pick any.

- Dry: understated, said with a straight face
- Laughing at myself: I'm usually the joke
- Corny: puns, wordplay, a good groan
- None: I keep humor out of my writing

Writes one line for each kind picked; None picked with anything else counts as None:
- Dry: "Where humor fits, keeps it dry: understated, said with a straight face, never pointed out."
- Laughing at myself: "Where humor fits, makes themselves the joke, never the reader."
- Corny: "Where humor fits, reaches for a pun or a bit of wordplay, and doesn't mind a groan."
- None: "Keeps jokes out of their writing."

The answers file records every option picked, in this order, separated by semicolons.

## W4 (typed)

> What would you hate to sound like? A word or phrase that makes you wince is perfect.

Feeds: `## Prohibitions`, as concrete phrases or moves; `about.md` `## Personality`.

### W4a (tapped)

Asked only when W4 names a kind of writing, such as "corporate" or "a finance textbook", rather than quoting words of it. Write three lines of that kind, one short sentence each, about the person's own work from W1, each a different phrase. They show the fault on purpose, so the floor does not hold them to it, and none has an em or en dash.

> Which of these would make you wince most?

- the first line
- the second line
- the third line
- None of these

Feeds: `## Prohibitions`, the line they tap as the example of what W4 names. None of these, or a skip, adds nothing beyond W4. The answers file records the three lines shown and the one tapped.

## Screen 2 (tapped)

Said before it, where it asks more than one question:

> A few more taps.

Where the samples left it only one:

> One more tap.

### P7. What they'd love to hear

> Someone just finished reading something you wrote. What would you most love them to say?

- "That was so clear."
- "You made me laugh."
- "I felt that."
- "OK, you've convinced me."

Writes:
- That was so clear: "Where a piece has room, aims first for it to be clear."
- You made me laugh: "Where a piece has room, aims first to make the reader laugh."
- I felt that: "Where a piece has room, aims first for the reader to feel it."
- OK, you've convinced me: "Where a piece has room, aims first to persuade."

### P8. Excitement

> You've got good news to share. Does your excitement show?

- Yes, let it show
- A little
- No, the news speaks for itself

Writes:
- Yes, let it show: "Lets their excitement show when they have good news."
- A little: "Shows a little excitement at good news, never more than the news."
- No, the news speaks for itself: "Lets good news speak for itself, with no added excitement."

### R1. How should your writing usually read?

- Easy and casual: like a text to a friend
- Everyday: clear to anyone outside your field
- Polished: careful, for readers who expect it
- Technical: for people who know the terms

"Depends" typed in the free-text answer is recorded as answered. Feeds: `## Reading Ease`, the option's level and the line under it, or on Depends or a skip, "Set by each piece's reader"; the routing row's register.

## Screen 3 (tapped)

Said before it:

> Last three, then I'll show you how you come across.

### P10. What they'd like their work known for

> To help me write the way you'd make a case: what would you most like your work to be known for?

- Getting real results
- Doing right by people
- Finding a better way
- Being someone people can count on

Writes:
- Getting real results: "When making a case, leads with what it gets done."
- Doing right by people: "When making a case, leads with what it means for the people involved."
- Finding a better way: "When making a case, leads with what is new or better about it."
- Being someone people can count on: "When making a case, leads with what is proven and dependable."

Also feeds the portrait's sentence on what drives them, and `about.md` `## Personality`.

### P11. The overrated virtue

> One just for fun, and it tells me a lot about your writing: which of these is a little overrated?

- Sounding certain
- Being thorough
- Being polished
- Being clever

Writes:
- Sounding certain: "Says where they are unsure rather than sound more certain than they are."
- Being thorough: "Leaves out what the reader doesn't need rather than cover everything."
- Being polished: "Lets a plain, human sentence stand rather than smooth it over."
- Being clever: "Takes the plain way to say a thing over the clever one."

### P12. Where readers would sit with them

> If your readers could sit down with you anywhere, where would feel most like you?

- A coffee shop
- A nice hotel lobby
- A design workshop
- A calm spa

Writes:
- A coffee shop: "Talks to the reader as an equal across a small table: relaxed, direct, no ceremony."
- A nice hotel lobby: "Treats the reader as a guest: courteous, attentive, and makes the next step easy."
- A design workshop: "Works ideas out alongside the reader: inventive, hands-on, and open about the work in progress."
- A calm spa: "Keeps an unhurried, reassuring pace; never rushes or alarms the reader."

Also feeds the portrait's sentence on how the people they write for experience them.

## A1. The portrait (tapped)

The question carries the portrait, so the two are always seen together: it opens with "Here's how you come across on the page:", then the portrait in full, then:

> Is that you on a good day?

- Yes, that's me
- Close, but...
- Not quite

On Close, but or Not quite, they say what to change, in the free-text answer or a reply. Feeds: the portrait; `about.md` `## Personality` where it changes their angle or their aim; where it says how they write rather than who they are, it is sorted like an A3 change.

## F2. The first piece (tapped)

Where W2 gave a point, F2 is not asked: the piece is the short post on what they wish people knew, and F2a asks who it is for, after this line:

> Now let's have a little fun with your first piece: a short post on the thing you wish more people knew.

Otherwise:

> Let's see it in action. What should I write first?

- Something I'm working on
- An email asking for a meeting
- A thank-you note to someone who helped

Skipped: a short note saying what they do, from W1; where W1 was skipped too, there is nothing to write from, and the run stops by `paths/coach.md` step 8.

## F2a (tapped for the post, typed otherwise)

For the post, a tap; its takeaway is W2's point, so it is not asked. Write the readers in three frames, filled from W1's own words, W2a counting as W2: first, the people W1 says come to them, or, where it names no one, the people who buy what they make or do; then "People looking to hire" someone who does their work; then "People curious about" their work. Name their work as W1 does. Where two frames would name the same people, show one. Never a group, a role or a place their answers give no sign of beyond these frames.

> Who should this post be for?

- [the first frame]
- [the second frame]
- [the third frame]
- Anyone: a general audience

Pick one. Each bracket stands for one frame, written as the paragraph above says; the bracket itself is never shown, and a frame not written is left out. Where fewer than two can be written, ask instead, typed: "Who should read it? A few words is plenty." The answers file records the readers shown and the one picked; typed in place of a tap, their words are the reader.

For every other piece, one sentence that gives the piece what it needs, typed, asked by the piece:
- Something I'm working on: "What is it, and who's it for? A sentence is plenty."
- The meeting email: "Who's it to, and what's the meeting about?"
- The thank-you note: "Who's it for, and what did they do?"

Skipped: write only from what they told you in W1 and W2, for someone who would follow their work; where W1 and W2 were skipped too, there is nothing to write from, and the run stops by `paths/coach.md` step 8. Feeds: the piece's subject and reader; `about.md` `## Current Focus` where it is their work, written by Onboard Root.

## Waiting line

Said once, as a message of its own, after F2a is answered or skipped, or after F2 is skipped, where a piece will be written; where a skip line stops the run instead, it is not said. Nothing of the piece is shown with it: the piece first appears with A2, after the second read.

> Writing your piece now. It takes a minute or two.

## A2. The first piece (typed)

The question carries the piece, so the two are always seen together: it opens with "Here's your first piece:", then the piece in full and, where anything in it was made up, the one line that says what, then this, typed, never as taps:

> First drafts are never quite right, and your pushback is how I learn your voice. What's wrong with it? Quote a line that doesn't sound like you and say it your way, or just tell me what's off.

Each piece opens in short paragraphs with a blank line between them. The answers file records each piece and what was made up. An answer that names a change is sorted by `paths/coach.md` step 6, even where it also praises the piece or says to send it. One that accepts the piece and names no change, such as "nothing", a bare "no" or "I'd send it", is their Yes: it sets the confirmation date, and the piece is not asked about again. One that says the piece is off but names nothing to change, such as "it's not me", is followed by A3. A skip or "not sure" is followed once by A2a. Feeds: each change by its sort in `paths/coach.md` step 6.

## A2a. The verdict on the first piece (tapped, only after a skip or "not sure")

Asked once, where A2 was skipped or answered "not sure", carrying the same piece as A2 does, then:

> Would you send this under your name?

- Yes, send it
- Almost: I'd change a few things
- No, it doesn't sound like me

Yes sets the confirmation date. Almost or No: A3. A skip stops the coach by `paths/coach.md` step 8.

## A3 (typed)

Asked after an A2 that says the piece is off but names nothing to change, or Almost or No at A2a, and as A5 on Almost or No at A4:

> What would you change? Quote the line and say it your way, or just tell me.

A skip or "not sure" here, or an answer that still names no change, stops the coach by `paths/coach.md` step 8, with no rewrite shown. Feeds: each change by its sort in `paths/coach.md` step 6.

## A3a (typed, only when they answer it)

Never asked as a question. Version 2 is shown with one line on where each change went, naming what is now in their voice and what stays in this piece, and ending:

> Tell me if any of those should go the other way.

Where they name one, typed in place of a tap or in a reply, it is recorded as A3a, or A5a on version 3, and moved: into the voice, one line in `## How They Write`, or in `## Prohibitions` where it says never; or out of it, so the piece keeps it and the voice does not. Then the question that version carries, A4 or A6, is asked again, and a move is not a return: it rewrites nothing, and no verdict is taken from the reply that names it. Silence moves nothing.

## A4. The verdict (tapped)

Asked with version 2, after the first rewrite, carrying the piece as A2 does: it opens with "Here it is with your changes:", then the piece in full and the one line A3a gives, then:

> Would you send this under your name?

- Yes, send it
- Almost: I'd change a few things
- No, it doesn't sound like me

Yes sets the confirmation date. Almost or No: A5, then the second rewrite, shown with A6. A skip or "not sure", here or at A6, stops the coach by `paths/coach.md` step 8.

## A6. Keep or stop (tapped)

Asked with the third version, after two returns, in place of A4, and carrying that version and its line as A4 does.

> Want to keep your voice as it is now, or stop here and pick it up later?

- Keep it
- Stop for now
