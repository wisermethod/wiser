# The eight fixed pairs

Situation for all eight: a short email to a colleague. Each pair carries one message written two ways that differ on one dimension only, and both sides pass the floor in `SKILL.md`. The dimension names are for the coach, never shown to the person.

Side order shown to the person alternates by pair number: odd pairs show A first, even pairs show B first. Show each passage whole, labeled A or B, with no dimension name.

## P1. Point first, or build up to it

**A, point first.**
Can you take Thursday's client call? Board prep has swallowed my week, and you know the account best after me. I'll send my notes tonight.

**B, build up.**
Board prep has swallowed my week, and you know the account best after me. Can you take Thursday's client call? I'll send my notes tonight.

## P2. A feeling named, or the facts named

**A, the feeling.**
Thank you for covering the Hendricks work while I was out. Coming back to nothing on fire meant a lot to me.

**B, the facts.**
Thank you for covering the Hendricks work while I was out. Both deliverables went out on time, and the client noticed nothing.

## P3. Sure, or careful

**A, sure.**
We should go with Northline. They answered every security question in writing and fit the budget. Tell me by Wednesday if you see a reason not to.

**B, careful.**
I think Northline is our best option. They answered every security question in writing and fit the budget, though we've seen only two references. Does anything give you pause?

## P4. Story, or proof

**A, story.**
Yesterday a new client stopped at question nine of our intake form, asked what it meant, and never finished. I think we should cut the form to five plain questions.

**B, proof.**
Eleven of forty new clients quit our intake form last quarter, most of them at question nine. I think we should cut the form to five plain questions.

## P5. Relaxed, or polished

**A, relaxed.**
Hey, could we push Tuesday's catch-up to Thursday? Something came up with the Denver team. Same time works for me, if it works for you too.

**B, polished.**
Would you be able to move Tuesday's meeting to Thursday? A matter with the Denver team needs my attention. The same time suits me, if it suits you.

## P6. Spark, or steady

**A, spark.**
The pilot worked! Response times dropped from two days to four hours. That's exactly what we hoped for, and I'd love to show the team on Friday.

**B, steady.**
The pilot worked. Response times dropped from two days to four hours. That matches what we set out to test, and I'd like to show the team on Friday.

## P7. A little wit, or straight

**A, wit.**
Expense reports are due Friday. Please type or scan your receipts: Finance can read most handwriting, but would rather not guess.

**B, straight.**
Expense reports are due Friday. Please type or scan your receipts, since handwritten ones slow Finance down and sometimes come back.

## P8. Short and punchy, or long and flowing

**A, short.**
Thank you for thinking of me for the committee. I have to say no this year. We launch two products before June. You'd get half my attention at best. Ask me again in the fall.

**B, long.**
Thank you for thinking of me for the committee. I have to say no this year, because we launch two products before June and you'd get half my attention at best, so ask me again in the fall.
