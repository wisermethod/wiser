# The eight fixed pairs

Situation for all eight: an email to a colleague. Each pair carries one message written two ways that differ on one dimension only, and both sides pass the floor in `SKILL.md`. The dimension names are for the coach, never shown to the person.

Side order shown to the person alternates by pair number: odd pairs show A first, even pairs show B first. Show each passage whole, labeled A or B, with no dimension name.

## P1. Point first, or build up to it

**A, point first.**
Can you take the Thursday client call? I've been pulled into the board prep and can't move it. You know the account better than anyone after me, and the agenda is short: the renewal terms and the March timeline. I'll send my notes tonight, and I'm free Wednesday at four if you want to walk through them.

**B, build up.**
I've been pulled into board prep this week, right on top of Thursday's client call, and you know the account better than anyone after me. The agenda is short: the renewal terms and the March timeline. Could you take the call? I'll send my notes tonight, and I'm free Wednesday at four to walk through them.

## P2. Close, or composed

**A, close.**
Thank you for picking up the Hendricks work last week. When I was out, you took it on without being asked, and I came back to nothing on fire. That meant a lot to me. I know you had your own deadlines. If you need cover next month, let me know and I will take it.

**B, composed.**
Thank you for covering the Hendricks work last week. Everything was current when I returned, including the two deliverables due Friday, which made the handover simple and meant the client noticed nothing. I know it came on top of your own deadlines. If you need cover next month, let me know and I will take it.

## P3. Sure, or careful

**A, sure.**
We should go with Northline. They were the only vendor who answered every security question in writing, both of their references renewed, and their price is inside our budget with room for training. I'd sign this week so we keep the January start. If you see a reason not to, tell me by Wednesday.

**B, careful.**
I think Northline is our best option. They were the only vendor who answered every security question in writing, and both references renewed, though two is a small sample. Their price fits the budget with some room for training. If we want the January start, signing this week would help. Does anything give you pause?

## P4. Story, or proof

**A, story.**
Yesterday I watched a new client fill out our intake form. She stopped at question nine, the one asking for her "engagement objective", and asked me what it meant. Then she closed the laptop and said she'd finish later. She didn't. I think we can cut the form to five plain questions. Can we try it this month?

**B, proof.**
Our intake form loses people. Of the forty clients who started it last quarter, eleven stopped before finishing, and eight of those stopped at question nine, the "engagement objective" field. I think we can cut the form to five plain questions. Can we try it this month and compare the completion rate in December?

## P5. Relaxed, or polished

**A, relaxed.**
Hey, could we push Tuesday's catch-up to Thursday? Something came up with the Denver team, and I'd rather give you my full attention than half of it. Same time works for me, or later if that's easier for you. I'm sorry to shuffle things around, and I appreciate you being flexible.

**B, polished.**
Would you be able to move Tuesday's meeting to Thursday? A matter with the Denver team requires my attention on Tuesday, and I would rather give our discussion my full focus. The same time suits me, or later in the day if that is more convenient. Thank you for accommodating the change.

## P6. Spark, or steady

**A, spark.**
The pilot worked! Response times dropped from two days to four hours, and three clients wrote in to say they noticed. That is exactly what we hoped for when we pitched this in June. I'd love to show the team on Friday and talk about rolling it out to everyone. Thank you for pushing for it.

**B, steady.**
The pilot worked. Response times dropped from two days to four hours, and three clients wrote in to say they noticed. That matches what we set out to test in June. I'd like to show the team on Friday and discuss rolling it out more widely. Thank you for pushing for it.

## P7. A little wit, or straight

**A, wit.**
Expense reports are due Friday. Please type or scan your receipts: Finance can read most handwriting, but not all of it, and they would rather not guess. If you're missing one, a note explaining what it was for is enough. Anything that arrives after Friday rolls into next month's cycle.

**B, straight.**
Expense reports are due Friday. Please attach typed or scanned receipts where you can, since handwritten ones slow Finance down and sometimes come back. If a receipt is missing, a short note explaining what it was for is enough. Anything that arrives after Friday rolls into next month's cycle.

## P8. Short and punchy, or long and flowing

**A, short.**
Thank you for thinking of me for the committee. I have to say no this year. My team launches two products before June, and I'd give you half my attention at best. That isn't fair to you or the work. Ask me again in the fall. I'd like to help then, and I mean it.

**B, long.**
Thank you for thinking of me for the committee. I have to say no this year, because my team launches two products before June and I would only be able to give you half my attention, which wouldn't be fair to the work. If you ask me again in the fall, I would like to help, and I mean that.
