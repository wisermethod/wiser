# Media Interview

## Scope

Spoken words for a journalist-led interview: a broadcast hit, a print interview, a podcast, a standup. The deliverable is the opening and the spoken answers the speaker will actually say, not a written Q&A pack (that is Content Author's `q-and-a.md`). This skill does not coach delivery.

## Length target

A broadcast hit is 3 to 5 minutes total, with answers of 15 to 30 seconds. A print or podcast interview runs 15 to 40 minutes; prepare 5 to 8 answers of 45 to 90 seconds plus a 20-second open. Word counts follow 130 to 150 words per minute for each answer, not for a single speech.

## Register

Conversational, on the record, short enough to clip without changing meaning. First person. One idea per answer. The journalist owns the questions; the speaker owns the answers.

## Ritual beats

- **Open, if the format has one.** 15 to 20 seconds: the one sentence the speaker came to say.
- **Answer, then bridge.** First sentence meets the question. Second sentence, if needed, carries the message. A third sentence is rare.
- **Proof.** One named fact, number, or scene per answer. Not a stack.
- **Stop.** Silence is how an answer ends. Filling it gives the journalist the next clip.

## Occasion-specific conventions

- Hostile questions are answered, not replaced. A duck becomes the clip.
- Hypotheticals are declined with a real fact: "I will not guess. Here is what we know."
- Repeating the journalist's loaded frame in the answer repeats the frame. Answer in the speaker's nouns.
- Applause architecture: none. A broadcast "hit" is not a speech.
- Opening line patterns that hold for a hosted open: the news in one sentence, then why it matters in one. Entering on "thanks for having me" spends the only seconds the audience hears before the first question.

## Anti-patterns

- **The speech.** A 90-second answer on a 20-second hit. The editor will cut; the speaker should have cut.
- **Agreeing to the premise to be polite.** "You're right that we failed, but..." has already given the headline.
- **Three messages in one answer.** The clip will be whichever clause is sharpest, not the one the speaker liked.
- **A written Q&A read aloud.** The sentences will sound like a document. Write for the ear, then stop.

## Notes on arc adaptation

There is no single throughline-to-gift speech. Each answer is a miniature: meet the question (anchor), one fact (build), the message (turn), stop (gift). The outline gate in the parent skill still runs: lock the one sentence the speaker came to say, and the 5 to 8 answers, before drafting prose.
