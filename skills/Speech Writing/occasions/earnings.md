# Earnings

## Scope

Prepared remarks for an earnings call, investor update, or results briefing, delivered by a named officer before analyst Q&A. This guide is for the scripted open. Q&A is not this skill.

## Length target

Duration range: 8 to 15 minutes. At 130 to 150 words per minute, that is 1,040 to 2,250 words. Most prepared remarks land at 10 to 12 minutes. Over 15 minutes collides with the Q&A the call exists to host.

## Register

Measured, specific, on the record. Numbers are spoken as numbers, not as adjectives. First person plural for the company; first person for a judgment the officer is actually making. Forward-looking claims carry their caution in the same sentence, not in a footer nobody hears.

## Ritual beats

- **Period and headline number.** What this call is, and the one figure that defines it.
- **What drove it.** Two or three causes, each with a number or a named product, not a theme.
- **What disappointed or is being watched.** One honest miss or risk, named. Skipping this makes the rest sound selected.
- **Outlook.** What is guided, in the language already cleared, and what is not being guided.
- **Hand-off to questions.**

## Occasion-specific conventions

- Every figure is spoken once, with its comparison period. "Up nicely" is not a number.
- Non-GAAP or adjusted figures are named as such when spoken, not only on the slide.
- Forward-looking statements are claims about the future; they stay inside what the company has already undertaken to say. This skill does not invent guidance.
- Applause architecture: none. This is a call, not a keynote.
- Opening line patterns that hold: entering on the period and the headline ("Third quarter revenue was $1.2 billion, up 6 percent from last year."), entering on the operational fact that drove the print. Entering on "what a year it's been" postpones the print.

## Anti-patterns

- **The highlight reel.** Only beats, no miss, no watch item. Analysts will ask; putting the miss in the remarks keeps the speaker from being introduced to it live.
- **Metaphor in place of a number.** "Tailwinds" and "headwinds" without the figure they stand in for.
- **Guidance by vibe.** "We feel good about the year" is not outlook.
- **A brand story in the middle.** Origin, purpose, and culture belong elsewhere unless they changed the print.

## Notes on arc adaptation

The universal arc applies with a short empathy anchor (the period and the print), a curiosity gap that is the miss or the watch item, a build that is drivers, a turn that is outlook, and a gift that is the hand-off. The long stage is the build of drivers, not a personal scene.
