# Testimony

## Scope

Oral testimony before a legislature, regulator, or formal hearing: an opening statement, usually five minutes, on the record, under oath or the equivalent. Written testimony submitted for the record is a document; if it is the deliverable, Content Author writes it and this occasion still governs the spoken open. This skill does not give legal advice and does not substitute for counsel.

## Length target

Duration range: 3 to 7 minutes unless the chair set a clock. At 130 to 150 words per minute, that is 390 to 1,050 words. A five-minute clock is 650 to 750 words. Running long is cut by the chair, not by applause.

## Register

Formal, precise, respectful of the body. First person. Short sentences. No jokes. Titles of members and of the body are correct; if the request does not supply them, ask.

## Ritual beats

- **Address and thanks, one line.** Chair and committee, not a paragraph of gratitude.
- **Who the speaker is, one line.** Role and why they were asked, not a resume.
- **The answer to the hearing's question.** The throughline is the answer, not a tour of the industry.
- **The facts that support it.** Numbered in speech only if the body asked for numbered points; otherwise spoken in order.
- **What the speaker asks of the body, if anything.** A specific action, or nothing.
- **Willingness to take questions.** The close.

## Occasion-specific conventions

- Answer the question the hearing was called to ask. A statement about a neighboring issue is non-responsive and will be treated as evasion.
- Counsel-cleared language stays counsel-cleared. Polishing it into a warmer register that changes the meaning is a defect.
- Citations are spoken as short names ("as we reported in March 2026"), not URLs.
- Applause architecture: none.
- Opening line patterns that hold: "Chair [Name], members of the committee, thank you. I am [role]. You asked whether [question]. The answer is [throughline]." Entering on a story before the answer spends the clock.

## Anti-patterns

- **The keynote.** A frame, a journey, a gift. This room asked a question.
- **Policy poetry.** "We must do better" with no asked-for action.
- **Arguing with a member in the open.** Disagreement with a premise is stated once, calmly, as a correction of fact. A duel belongs in Q&A, and even there it is a fact correction.
- **Filling the clock with biography.** The record already has the bio.

## Notes on arc adaptation

The universal arc is overridden. The stages are address, identity, answer, support, ask, questions. Empathy-anchor scenes are not the open unless the hearing is itself about that scene and counsel has cleared it. The throughline is the answer to the body's question.
