# Funnel Types

Reference material for funnel selection and stage structure. Covers six funnel patterns, when each fits, the stages it runs, what it measures, and how each one most often fails, then the criteria that map a business model to a pattern. `SKILL.md` consults this file at type selection and stage architecture; this skill is the library's single home.

Every price point, range, and metric named here is a general industry pattern, unverified against any particular market (`standards/conventions.md`). They are starting points, replaced by the requester's own data wherever it exists.

## Awareness

**Fits** a new product or a new category, where the audience does not yet know the problem exists or that anything solves it.

**Stages**

1. Content that surfaces the problem (articles, video, social)
2. A lead magnet (guide, checklist, tool) that captures interest
3. A nurture sequence that educates and builds trust
4. A soft offer (demo, trial, consultation) once trust exists

**Measures:** reach, engagement rate, lead magnet conversion rate, nurture to qualified rate.

**Fails when** the offer arrives early. This pattern earns attention before it asks for anything, and an ask before trust is where the audience leaves.

## Lead Generation

**Fits** an established business with a clear offer and a defined audience that already knows the problem and is looking for solutions.

**Stages**

1. A targeted landing page with a clear value proposition
2. Lead capture (form, quiz, calculator)
3. Qualification, scored automatically or by hand
4. Sales handoff, or direct conversion

**Measures:** landing page conversion rate, cost per lead, lead quality score, sales-accepted lead rate.

**Fails when** it optimizes for volume over quality. A form that captures a thousand unqualified leads is worse than one that captures a hundred qualified ones, because the cost lands downstream where nobody is counting.

## Product Launch

**Fits** a new product, feature, or service going to an audience or list that already exists.

**Stages**

1. Pre-launch buzz (teaser content, waitlist, early access)
2. A launch sequence carrying urgency, across email, social, and paid
3. The core offer page, with social proof, demonstration, and pricing
4. Follow-up that answers objections
5. A close on a deadline, a bonus, or genuine scarcity

**Measures:** waitlist size, launch-day conversions, conversion rate by source, revenue per subscriber.

**Fails when** the scarcity is invented. A product that is always available should not pretend to be limited; audiences detect it, and the trust does not come back for the next launch.

## Webinar or Workshop

**Fits** a high-consideration offer where the audience needs education before it can buy. Common in business-to-business, coaching, and consulting.

**Stages**

1. A registration page naming the outcome, the time, and why the speaker is worth an hour
2. A reminder sequence that protects attendance
3. The live event, education first, with a natural turn to the offer
4. Replay and follow-up for the people who missed it or have not decided
5. A close tied to the event, time-limited

**Measures:** registration rate, attendance rate, offer conversion rate, replay conversion rate.

**Fails when** the event is a pitch wearing a lesson's clothes. Attendees who feel tricked do not convert, and they tell other people.

## Tripwire

**Fits** a business that needs cold traffic to become buyers quickly. The tripwire is a low-price, high-value offer whose job is to break the buyer barrier, commonly in the low tens of dollars, roughly $7 to $47.

**Stages**

1. An ad or content driving to the low-price offer
2. A purchase page built for impulse, with minimal friction
3. An upsell sequence immediately after purchase
4. Nurture toward the core product, now addressed to a customer rather than a lead

**Measures:** tripwire conversion rate, average order value including upsells, customer lifetime value, tripwire to core conversion rate.

**Fails when** the tripwire has nothing to do with the core offer. It has to be a stepping stone: someone who buys it should find the core offer obvious rather than unrelated.

## Application

**Fits** premium or high-touch services where the business selects its clients. Consulting firms, agencies, coaching programs, enterprise software.

**Stages**

1. Content that establishes expertise and results
2. An application page carrying qualifying questions and the investment level
3. Review and selection by the business
4. A discovery call, then onboarding

**Measures:** application rate, qualification rate, close rate, client lifetime value.

**Fails when** the application does not actually filter. If every applicant is accepted, it is a lead form performing exclusivity, and the qualification the pattern exists to provide never happens.

## Selection Criteria

| Business model | Primary funnel | Why |
|----------------|----------------|-----|
| High-ticket services | Application or Webinar | Long consideration cycle, trust required before a conversation |
| Enterprise software | Lead Generation or Webinar | Qualification matters, several decision-makers |
| Product-led or self-serve software | Awareness or Tripwire | Low friction, volume oriented |
| E-commerce, new brand | Awareness, then Lead Generation | Build the brand before selling against it |
| E-commerce, established | Lead Generation or Tripwire | The audience already knows the category |
| Coaching or consulting | Webinar or Application | Trust and qualification are both critical |
| Information products and courses | Product Launch or Webinar | The purchase is education-driven |
| Marketplace or platform | Awareness, supply side first | Chicken and egg; one side has to be solved before the other |
