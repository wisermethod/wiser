Read only for `paths/brief.md`, `paths/simple.md` and `paths/record-my-look.md`. The full sitting uses `paths/sitting.md` and `lock.md`, which replace the old stages and their confirmation rules.

## Sentences the person adopts

Does this skill need a draft of the promise, the brand sentence at B4 or the photo sentence at B12 in `questions.md` The brief path, or sample copy for a board? Yes: is a voice bound and available, as `skills/Content Author/` Inputs require? Yes: first decide whether `experts/Ghost Writer/`'s scope test applies to this sentence, before any draft. A sentence the person may adopt into `design.md` or say as their answer to "what do you do?" is in scope; sample copy that only fills a board is not. In scope: run `skills/Content Author/` by name in that bound `voice`, handing the owning root, the reader who asked "what do you do?" and has not met the brand, the sentence's use and length, its placement in `design.md` and the boards if adopted, and the claim it must land in `<user_request>`, and the person's own answers with their numbers in `<source_material>`; its Ghost Writer gate must pass the draft before the person sees it. Not in scope: write the sample line here from their numbered answers, mark it sample, and record that the gate was not applicable. No available voice: offer the person's own words, labelled as such, or a draft written here from their numbered answers alone and marked as a working draft, not in their voice, and say voice did not inform it; never run Content Author without its required input. No draft needed: retain the person's chosen words with their source. Test the brand sentence as the answer they would give to "what do you do?", or for a company "what does your company do?". Is it something they would be proud to say? Yes: offer it for their choice. No: return the need to Content Author where voice is available, or ask for their own words. Which wording do they choose? One offered wording: record it and its status; adopted wording may become the rule. Their own wording: record it as their words; it may become the rule. Neither or no answer: leave it unconfirmed; an unchosen draft never becomes a rule.

## Voice check

Run this check wherever the retained paths choose words, before accepting them. Is `voice.md` real and does an answered rule govern these words? Yes: compare them. Do they disagree? Yes: quote both sides to the person, citing the voice file and the proposed words' answer numbers, then load and ask F9 in `questions.md` Retained brief voice comparison and use only its outcomes. Keep the comparison and decision at F9; a kept writing rule leaves the visual requirement it set aside out of `design.md`. No disagreement: retain the sourced words and record F9's no-disagreement outcome. No governing voice rule: record F9's unavailable-comparison outcome. An unresolved brief choice follows its budget and labels Words only if empty. A stub or absent voice is said once at the close: voice did not inform the brand. Never edit `voice.md` to resolve a disagreement.

## Write and read back

Write for the skills that read it: rules first, short, one plain sentence per rule where one will do. Use the template's headings, including any optional heading it carries; keep its standing lines and replace its subject placeholder from the resolved root. Replace every prompt with supported rules; use an evidence label where a heading is empty and for failed claims as the separate pass below says. Write Layout Habits from P1 and P4; use supplied icon and graphic rules or the simple board, with unanswered headings labelled. State the chosen sentence and core words themselves, the actual colour values and typeface names, and the picks as usable rules, each with its answer number in place as `records.md` says. A produced palette, system or confirmed guide also cites its output file; a pointer to an answer never stands in for its result. Include no account of the interview or how a source was read; a reading's limits belong in the answers record, in the row of the question that source answered, beside the source. On the brief path, aim for about 400 words or fewer.
