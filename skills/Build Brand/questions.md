## The five questions

These questions choose how your brand should look, not who you are. Where `about.md` has an answered personality, name it once before P1 in at most six plain words total, with no filename or parenthetical; never repeat it at each question. The answers select its visible expression. No answered personality: omit that introduction; on the full path the Playbook instance's Witness records the absence, as `SKILL.md` Inputs says. Do not ask for personality again or derive a personality trait from a visual pick.

The five axes separate the age of the forms, their visual force, their colour temperature, their sense of play, and how they appear to have been made, so each can change colour, lettering, shape, or imagery without forcing the others. The combined warm-and-organic versus cool-and-precise candidate is separated into P3's temperature and P5's construction: warm can be geometric, and cool can be hand-shaped. Spacious versus dense is left out because the amount of room and information follows the use, such as a working screen, a page drawing attention, or a page for reading, rather than a permanent trait of the brand; record that use from the brief and apply the appropriate layout later.

Before P1, say once: "Middle balances both; neither leaves it open." For every P question, show the two poles with equal prominence, followed by **middle** and **neither**, without repeating their explanation or the pole names as a separate choices list. Retain an answer already documented for that axis without asking it again; otherwise the offered default is **neither**, said only where helpful. Do not infer a default from an image without labelling it a proposal for the person to accept. Each P entry repeats its default so it can be used on its own. Both paths use these questions in words.

Every handoff to `skills/Color Palette Design/` and `skills/Typography Design/` puts **brand personality as `<design_context>`**, read from the answered part of `about.md`, together with the cited P answer and existing constraints. No answered personality: state its absence and hand over only the cited visual choice. The per-axis feeds below refine that context; they do not manufacture traits to fit a table. A supplied colour remains the hue anchor, a required typeface remains a stated constraint, and any disagreement with a new pick is recorded for reconciliation. Hue-Personality Mapping supplies tendencies when no colour anchors the hue; the visual axes alone do not select a hue range or a named typeface. Layout scale still follows usage context.

### P1. Classic or contemporary

**In words, ask:** "Should your brand look classic or contemporary?"

This sets whether lettering and layouts use older or newer forms.

- **Classic:** Familiar letter shapes and proportions.
- **Contemporary:** Simpler letter shapes and newer proportions.
- **Choices:** middle; neither.
- **Default:** neither.
- **Fills:** Visual Personality; Typography; Layout Habits.
- **Time and skip:** about ten seconds to choose, plus reading; skipped choice: empty answer, status skipped; label only in `design.md`.

**Feeds:** Visual Personality expresses the `about.md` description through a chosen sense of continuity or currency. Color Palette Design receives that as brand personality in `<design_context>`, with no hue implied by age. Typography Design receives the same context for its Font Archetypes: classical proportions and finishing strokes can support Traditional, authoritative; simplified forms can support Geometric, precise or Humanist, approachable, only where the rest of the brief agrees.

### P2. Quiet or bold

**In words, ask:** "Should your brand look quiet and restrained or bold and loud?"

This sets how strong your colours and lettering look.

- **Quiet and restrained:** Muted colour and lighter emphasis.
- **Bold and loud:** Stronger colour and heavier emphasis.
- **Choices:** middle; neither.
- **Default:** neither.
- **Fills:** Visual Personality; Color; Typography.
- **Time and skip:** about ten seconds to choose, plus reading; skipped choice: empty answer, status skipped; label only in `design.md`.

**Feeds:** Visual Personality expresses the `about.md` description through a chosen level of visible force. Color Palette Design receives brand personality in `<design_context>` plus this choice for chroma, meaning colour strength, and accent distribution, not a new primary hue. Typography Design receives it for weight hierarchy and the degree of display emphasis; its scale ratio still follows domain context, rather than treating loud as a request for a larger ratio everywhere.

### P3. Warm or cool

**In words, ask:** "Should your colours look warm or cool?"

This sets your palette's starting colour family.

- **Warm:** Yellows, oranges or reds, including pale surfaces.
- **Cool:** Blues or blue-greens, including pale surfaces.
- **Choices:** middle; neither.
- **Default:** neither.
- **Fills:** Visual Personality; Color; Imagery and Illustration.
- **Time and skip:** about ten seconds to choose, plus reading; skipped choice: empty answer, status skipped; label only in `design.md`.

**Feeds:** Visual Personality records a preferred colour temperature alongside the `about.md` description, without adding a claim of friendliness or precision. Color Palette Design receives brand personality in `<design_context>` and the temperature preference alongside its Hue-Personality Mapping: use the actual supplied personality words to select a tendency, state any deliberate departure, and retain a supplied primary hue unless a change was requested. Typography Design receives the same context as a specimen colour constraint; temperature alone chooses no Font Archetypes row or scale ratio.

### P4. Playful or serious

**In words, ask:** "Should your brand look playful or serious?"

This sets how much surprise or humour appears.

- **Playful:** Unexpected shapes, movement or visual jokes.
- **Serious:** Steady alignments and direct images.
- **Choices:** middle; neither.
- **Default:** neither.
- **Fills:** Visual Personality; Typography; Layout Habits; Imagery and Illustration.
- **Time and skip:** about ten seconds to choose, plus reading; skipped choice: empty answer, status skipped; label only in `design.md`.

**Feeds:** Visual Personality expresses the `about.md` description through an allowance for visible surprise or a preference for direct presentation. Color Palette Design receives brand personality in `<design_context>` with this allowance, not an instruction to equate playful with bright or serious with dark. Typography Design receives it for letter behaviour and Font Archetypes character, such as Humanist, approachable or Technical, sharp where the other answers support them; weight and scale remain independent. Imagery rules record whether visual humour belongs.

### P5. Hand-shaped or geometric

**In words, ask:** "Should your brand look made by hand or built from exact geometric shapes?"

This sets whether shapes and lettering use hand-drawn or measured forms.

- **Made by hand:** Small intentional variations in outlines and marks.
- **Geometric:** Measured curves, straight edges and matching repeated shapes.
- **Choices:** middle; neither.
- **Default:** neither.
- **Fills:** Visual Personality; Typography; Imagery and Illustration; Mark as preparation only.
- **Time and skip:** about ten seconds to choose, plus reading; skipped choice: empty answer, status skipped; label only in `design.md`.

**Feeds:** Visual Personality expresses the `about.md` description through a rule about irregular or measured construction. Color Palette Design receives brand personality in `<design_context>` with that rule for applications, with no hue or chroma implied by construction. Typography Design receives the same context for Font Archetypes characteristics, such as open counters and rounded terminals under Humanist, approachable or even spacing and clean geometry under Geometric, precise; hand-shaped does not require script lettering. The mark stage receives only a form constraint, never a claim that a mark has been made.
