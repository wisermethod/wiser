# Op-Ed

A bylined argument published under a named author's name, read by people who chose an opinion page, not a news brief. Read this file at Frame and hold it through Draft. A news announcement in the issuer's name is `press-release.md`. A spoken keynote is `skills/Speech Writing/`.

Review expert: `experts/Ghost Writer/`. This root carries no news-desk reviewer, so whether an opinion page would run it is not judged here; name that gap.

## Shape

- **Length.** 600 to 800 words, the band most opinion pages still assign. Under 500 is a letter. Over 900 needs the page's own longer slot, confirmed, not assumed.
- **Structure.** A timely hook, the claim, the argument in two or three turns, the concession that makes the claim honest, the close that asks the reader to see or do one thing.
- **Paragraphs.** Two to four sentences. Print and web opinion both punish walls.
- **Stance.** First person. The byline is the point. "One might argue" is someone else's piece.

## Format rules

- **One claim.** A sentence the headline could carry. Two claims is two op-eds.
- **Why this author.** The piece has to be one this person can uniquely sign: a role, a result, a scene they were in. Credentials in a bio line, not in the opening.
- **Why now.** A hook dated in the last news cycle, a vote, a filing, a number that just landed. An evergreen lecture is not an op-ed.
- **Evidence in the body.** One number, one scene, or one named case per turn. A turn with none is a sermon.
- **Concession.** The strongest objection, answered. An op-ed that never admits a cost reads as a release.

## Failure modes

- **The announcement in costume.** "Company X is proud to" with a personal pronoun swapped in. If the organization could issue it as a release, it is a release.
- **Lecture with no hook.** Opening on the history of the issue. The news cycle is the door; history is the furniture.
- **Both-sides throat-clearing.** Spending the first third on how complicated it all is. State the claim, then earn it.
- **A close that restates.** The last paragraph repeats the lede. End on the action or the reframe.

## Format checks

- One claim, nameable in a headline.
- The author is the only person who could have signed this draft.
- A dated hook sits in the opening, not in paragraph four.
- Ghost Writer review ran, and any news-desk gap was named.
