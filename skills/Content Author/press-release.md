# Press Release

A news announcement issued in the name of the person or organization announcing it, read first by a journalist on deadline, then possibly posted for the public. Read this file at Frame and hold it through Draft. A one-to-one email asking a journalist to cover something is `media-pitch.md`. A spoken opening at a press conference is `skills/Speech Writing/` with `occasions/press-conference.md`.

Review expert: `experts/Ghost Writer/`. This root carries no news-desk reviewer, so whether a desk would take the story is not judged here; name that gap rather than reading the voice verdict as one.

## Shape

- **Length.** 400 to 500 words. Under 300 the news was thin or this is a statement. Over 600 is a feature, not a release.
- **Structure.** Headline, dateline, lede, nut graf, one or two quotes, supporting facts, boilerplate.
- **Paragraphs.** Two to three short sentences. The journalist is skimming, not settling in.
- **Stance.** Third person. The issuer is named, never "we", unless a quote.

## Format rules

- **Headline.** What happened, in about ten words, with a verb. Not a slogan and not a topic label.
- **Lede.** The news in the first sentence: who did what, when, where it matters. A reader who stops after that sentence still has the story.
- **Nut graf.** Why it matters, in the second paragraph, before color or background.
- **Quotes.** One from a named, titled speaker, in that person's voice, saying something a reporter cannot paraphrase from the lede. A second quote only when it adds a distinct fact or reaction. No adjective stacks ("delighted", "thrilled", "excited to announce").
- **Boilerplate.** Last, one short paragraph of who the issuer is. Not the news.
- **Dateline.** City and date on the first body line, in the form the issuer's house style already uses.

## Failure modes

- **Marketing lede.** Opening on a benefit, a vision, or "today announced" with no news underneath. Test: would a desk editor still have the story if the first sentence were deleted? If yes, the news is buried.
- **No news.** A product exists, a hire happened last quarter, a partnership has no consequence. If the journalist cannot answer "why today", this is not a release.
- **Quote that restates the lede.** The speaker says the headline back in first person. Cut it or give them a fact only they can say.
- **Keyword stuffing.** Search phrases forced into the headline and lede. The reader is a journalist, not a crawler.

## Format checks

- The first sentence carries who, what, and why now.
- Headline uses a verb and could not sit on a competitor's release unchanged.
- Every quote is attributable to a named person with a title, and none is only an adjective.
- Length sits inside the band.
- Ghost Writer review ran, and any news-desk gap was named.
