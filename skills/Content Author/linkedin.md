# LinkedIn Post

A post on a professional network feed, read on a phone by someone scrolling between other things and deciding in a second whether to stop. Read this file at Frame and hold it through Draft. Shorter public posts on an open social feed are `social-post.md`; a published article is `blog-post.md`.

## Shape

- **Length.** 150 to 300 words, roughly 800 to 1,200 characters for a standard post and up to about 2,100 for a piece doing heavier thinking.
- **Structure.** The hook line, then four to ten short paragraphs, then a close that hands over a takeaway or asks a question worth answering.
- **Paragraphs.** One to three sentences, with a line break between them. A single-sentence paragraph is normal here. Dense blocks get scrolled past rather than read.
- **Sentences.** Short, averaging around eight to twelve words. This is a mobile format and complexity is read as friction rather than as depth.
- **Stance.** First person, peer to peer, professional without being corporate. A company-blog register on a personal feed reads as marketing and is discounted as marketing.
- **One insight.** A post carrying three insights lands none of them. Pick the one and develop it.

## The fold

The feed cuts the post after its first lines, somewhere between 120 and 210 characters depending on the reader's device, and the tap to expand is itself a signal the platform counts. Write so the hook is complete inside the first 120 characters: those characters are the whole pitch for everything after them, and they deserve a disproportionate share of the effort spent on the post. `openings.md` carries the craft.

## Format rules

- **Ground the insight in one specific thing:** a number, an observed moment, something that happened on a particular day. A personal story carrying a concrete number does more here than either does alone.
- **Hashtags: skip them.** They no longer route anything on this platform, a handful at the end is inert, and a wall of them reads as spam.
- **Links.** An external link in the body suppresses how far the post travels. Post without it, or name the resource in the body and put the URL in the first comment, which costs conversion and keeps reach. A link to a video hosted elsewhere is the worst of both; upload video to the platform instead.
- **Choose the format before writing.** A document carousel, a poll, or native video travels further than plain text, and plain text is the fallback when the material fits nothing else. A carousel runs around a dozen vertical slides at 25 to 50 words each and teaches something; a product pitch in carousel form is the one that does not travel. Native video runs 30 to 60 seconds, vertical, uploaded rather than linked.
- **Emoji.** None, unless the bound voice uses them.
- **Close on a takeaway or a real question,** one you would want answered. "Agree?" and "Thoughts?" are engagement bait and are read as bait.

## Platform mechanics

- Saves and comments count for more than likes, and a comment carrying a real sentence counts for more than a short one, so the post to aim at is one worth keeping and worth answering.
- The first hour decides distribution. A post that draws nothing in that hour does not get a second wind, so publish when you can reply to comments as they arrive rather than hours later.
- Tuesday through Thursday mornings in the reader's time zone outperform; weekends and late evenings are dead.
- Space posts at least a day apart and vary the format, since the same format three times running is throttled.
- A post that works keeps resurfacing for two to three weeks, which favors material that stays true over material tied to the week's news.

## Failure modes

- **The announcement opening.** "Thrilled to announce" and its family spend the fold on throat-clearing, and the fold is all there is.
- **Humble-brag framing.** "I never expected this, but." The reader recognizes the shape and discounts what follows it.
- **Generic advice.** An insight the reader has scrolled past a hundred times. Test: is there anything here that only you could have written?
- **Corporate voice creep.** Test: would you say this sentence to a peer over coffee? If not, rewrite the thought rather than the words.
- **Manufactured contrarianism.** Forced disagreement, invented controversy, and engagement tricks. A real observation does not need them, and the tricks are recognized faster than the observation is.
- **Buried lead.** Context first, insight at the third paragraph, on the far side of the fold, where only the already committed will find it.
- **Template sameness.** Every post built on the same skeleton. A person writing has natural variation; a template does not.
- **Wrong format.** Writing plain text when the material was a carousel or a poll. That choice comes before the draft, not after it.

## Format checks

- The hook is complete inside 120 characters and carries the insight, not the setup.
- One insight, grounded in something specific.
- Paragraphs run one to three sentences with breaks between them.
- Length sits inside the band above.
- No hashtags, no engagement bait, no emoji the bound voice does not use.
- Any link sits in the first comment, not the body.
- Read cold, it sounds like a person talking to peers.
