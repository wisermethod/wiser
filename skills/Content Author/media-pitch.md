# Media Pitch

An outreach email to one journalist who did not ask for it, making one case that this is their story. Read this file at Frame and hold it through Draft. A public announcement is `press-release.md`. Who to pitch, and whether it is a story at all, is a targeting judgment no primitive in this root makes; this file writes the email once the requester has decided that.

Review expert: `experts/Ghost Writer/`, which judges the sentences. This root carries no news-desk reviewer and makes no targeting judgment; name both gaps rather than reading the voice verdict as either.

## Shape

- **Length.** 150 to 250 words. Over 300 is a release pasted into mail.
- **Structure.** Subject line, one-line why-you, the news, why now, one proof, one ask.
- **Paragraphs.** One to three sentences. This is read on a phone between other pitches.
- **Stance.** Direct, specific, peer to the desk. Not a campaign and not a press release with a greeting on top.

## Format rules

- **One journalist.** The body names why this desk, this beat, or this recent piece makes them the reader. A pitch that could go to fifty people unchanged is not a pitch.
- **Subject line.** The news, under about 60 characters. Not "quick question", not the announcing organization's name alone, not a tease that hides the story.
- **Why now.** A date, a filing, a number, a news cycle the desk is already in. Without it the mail waits.
- **One ask.** An interview, an embargoed look, a fact check, a copy of the report. Not "would love to connect".
- **Assets.** Offer one, named: the release, a data table, a spokesperson, a photo. Do not attach a kit unless the journalist asked.

## Failure modes

- **Blast copy.** First name mail-merged onto a generic body. Test: could the subject line and first two lines go to another beat unchanged? Then it is not about them.
- **The release in the body.** Pasting the announcement and adding "thought this might interest you". The pitch has to argue the story, not deliver the inventory.
- **Three asks.** Interview and exclusive and a feature. Pick one.
- **Flattery with no beat.** Praising the journalist's work without tying it to this news. Cut it.

## Format checks

- The subject line states the news.
- The first lines say why this journalist, not why this organization.
- One ask, one proof, a why-now that a desk could check.
- Length sits inside the band.
- Ghost Writer review ran, and any news-desk and targeting gaps were named.
